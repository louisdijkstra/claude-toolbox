Statusline layouts

Ten ways to structure the line, each rendered at the size and colours it will actually have. The hairline on the right of every panel marks column 80, so anything crossing it wraps in a standard terminal.

Every option is shown in the three states the line has to survive: a plain repository, a worktree, and a plan with rolling limits instead of a monthly budget. The reset countdown travels with whichever meter it belongs to — the monthly budget rolls over at the start of next month, the five-hour and weekly windows on their own clocks.

3

Hairline

No icons, no bars. Thin dividers, one colour for what changes, grey for everything else. Resets are written as plain remaining time.

worktree

Opus 1M │ my-app/libs/my-app-core │ main │ ctx 45% │ 5h 22%, 2h30m left │ 7d 61%, 4d6h left

Cost: no bars means no peripheral read — you have to parse the number to know you are at 90%.

9

Bracketed

The tmux convention: each field in brackets, no colour needed to separate them. Reads correctly even piped somewhere that strips ANSI.

repo

[Opus 1M] [my-app/libs/my-app-core] [main] [ctx 45%] [$879 ↻20d11h]

worktree

[Opus 1M] [.claude 🌳 budget-ledger] [feature-x] [ctx 45%] [$879 ↻20d11h]

rolling limits

[Opus 1M] [my-app/libs/my-app-core] [main] [ctx 45%] [5h 22% ↻2h30m] [7d 61% ↻4d6h]

Cost: twelve brackets of pure punctuation on a rolling plan, and it looks like every other tmux bar.

Every option carries the same fields: model and context size, location, branch, context window, and either the monthly budget or the two rolling windows. What changes is structure, density and how much attention the line asks for.

Monthly budget
Spend against the cap, resetting at the start of next month — shown here as ↻20d11h.
Rolling limits
A five-hour window and a weekly one, each with its own countdown. Both are live at once, so two resets share the line.

Constraints they all share: the hook gets no terminal width, emoji occupy two columns and align unreliably, and anything past column 80 wraps.
